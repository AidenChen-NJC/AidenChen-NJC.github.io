Can AI replace consultants?AI 能取代顧問嗎?

I don't think it can. Human trust, credibility, and being an industry insider are why AI replaces the tasks, not the job. So I built a product to automate my tasks — and the real lesson was learning what to delete.我不認為它能。人的信任、可信度、業內身分,是 AI 只能取代任務、無法取代職位的原因。所以我做了一個產品來自動化我的任務——而真正的收穫,是學會該刪掉什麼。

I think it can't. Human trust, credibility, and being an industry insider are all good reasons why it can't replace the job, only the tasks.

That being said, the demand for a human gatekeeper for AI output is much lower than the current workforce, so I still think AI is replacing many junior roles.

Let's build a product to automate my tasks.

time to lock in

Step One: Context

Step 1 is understanding the context. As a junior consultant, my day-to-day work is usually:

  • Trying to answer an ambiguous, extremely hard question. (e.g., Is the Y market ready for our client to enter?)
  • Test a hypothesis. (e.g., “Regulations are the main barrier for our client to launching X product”)

The Human Way

In consulting, we generally solve these two types of questions by these steps:

  1. Define problem
  2. structure problem
  3. prioritize issue
  4. develop analysis
  5. conduct analysis
  6. synthesize findings
  7. develop recommendations

(Google search “McKinsey 7-step problem solving” for more info, a lot of decent explanations online, e.g., slideworks.io/resources/mckinsey-problem-solving-process)

The AI Way

Can a company put $500M into a new product launch just because Opus 4.8 says to do it? I don't think so. Thus, I think the gap comes down to one thing only: trust. Client recommendations are provided by consulting firms such as McKinsey and BCG. They have their reputation and credibility. Thus, AI work needs to be proofread, interpreted, and refined by skillful consultants.

Thus, human judgment remains the KSF (key success factor) here; I want to design a workflow that follows a human approach and serves as an assistant in my work.

Product Thesis & Scope

Pain point: I don't trust AI output because its reasoning process is a black box.

Solution: Break that black box into steps a human can see and review. and let AI do the research.

Scope: I mentioned that my tasks are testing a hypothesis & trying to find an answer to an ambiguous question. However, for this product, I focus only on testing the hypothesis, meaning the input will be statements, not questions, because these two tasks have very different scopes. Answering questions needs clarification and a lot of back and forth with real people, so I will only be focusing on decomposing a statement and finding the true and false behind the statement.

Step Two: Let's Cook

Pasted all the context from step 1 into Claude Code and told it to create this product. Tested it, and three problems pop up:

Problem

1. The decomposition/definition of the question to be answered isn't concrete enough, or I don't agree with it.

e.g.,

Problem Statement: Is the Y market ready for our client's entry?

AI decomposition of the problem:

  • Is the market big enough?
  • Is it market penetrable?
  • penetration forecast

My Comment:

  • Looks sensible at first glance, but the scope of the forecast is ambiguous.
  • For me, forecasting penetration equals to answering the two questions (market size & penetration).
  • Other than size and % forecast, there are also qualitative factors in answering “Is the Y market ready for our client to enter?”, e.g., regulations? opportunity cost?

2. The evidence is fake. (hallucinated URL)

3. The evidence is real but not comprehensive. (for instance, revenue growth doesn't equal profitability growth, but AI might catch revenue growth data and come to the “profitability growth” conclusion.)

Solution

Solution to Problem 1:

Hard facts: Reasoning-wise, I think I'm still better than AI. Even if not, AI still has to convince me; thus, regardless of who is better, AI & me need alignment.

Solution: build a highly interactive interface; decompose layer by layer, with each layer requiring my approval to continue.

  • build a mindset.md file to introduce the consulting way (i.e., issue tree, hypo tree, etc) of problem solving.
  • build a memory.md file to train AI on how to reason like me by dumping casebooks into the file, and test-run several rounds with me.
  • build a lesson.md file, a learning loop, but not an append-only file. After every human correction, record the mindset and principle to keep it lean, avoiding it from being an infinitely growing log.

Problem 2:

Initial thought: Add a step to verify each URL's authenticity one by one, and check its quote word for word.

After test: I ran it on a real argument (“remote work lowers productivity”), and a PubMed source came back “dead”, not because it was fake, but because PubMed blocks scrapers. Naive verification was about to kill a real Tier-A academic source.

Fix: distinguish “site blocks crawlers” (PubMed, doi.org) from “actually doesn't exist” by adding a browser-like header, retry mirror sources before declaring dead. Lesson: “I couldn't fetch it,” ≠ “it doesn't exist,” and that error is most dangerous on the most authoritative sources.

Problem 3:

Solution is simple: Force answers to use verbatim source text only, no rephrasing allowed.

End Product

The Module I built but then killed

Additionally, I want to learn a lesson that I've learned along the way.

My mind stops at designing the input and lets AI do the research. It led me to accidentally design an auto-rollup: AI reads all the verdicts and research conclusions and rolls them into one overall answer. The summary rebuilds another black box at the end.

What replaced it: set it to allow only live URLs and real quotes; no rephrasing allowed. Instead, I add a confidence scoring system for the AI to label each answer with its confidence in how it thinks its answer is relevant to the question and can actually answer my question, even though it's also a blackbox process, but I read them myself, and the confidence index just helps me more in identifying where it could potentially go wrong.

Takeaway: Delete-first. Elon Musk once shared this sauce in an interview. Break down your work into atomic modules, then try deleting them all. Only add the module back when the answer to “Will the product collapse without this module?” is YES. This is extremely useful in my vibecoding journey because I feel like, with /plan and /ultraplan becoming mainstream, and vibecoding, AI sometimes includes some really cool features in your design, but also some fancy BS.

Lesson learned

You have to know what you are building. You are the gatekeeper in the AI loop. Otherwise, the loop is just garbage in; bigger garbage out. /plan and /ultraplan are powerful commands, but AI will wrap some BS and call it a feature if you don't have alignment with it.

Delete-first. Break your work plan into atomic working modules. Try deleting each one, and only add it back if the product won't work otherwise. Make the process as lean as possible. Don't install fancy CLAUDE.md files or skills on GitHub. Only do it when you know exactly what you need it for; then you will realize you might as well create your own. GitHub repos are like Pinterest; they inspire you, not a free plug-and-play tool.

Consultants who can ship, iterate, and grow AI products/workflows for clients are the future of consulting. We have an overarching view of the organization to know what to build, why build it, and what problems it solves. These are all way more important than building itself.

那就來做一個產品,把我的任務自動化吧。

進入專注模式。

第一步:脈絡(Context)

第 1 步是搞懂脈絡。身為一個 junior 顧問,我的日常工作通常是:

  • 試著回答一個模糊、極度困難的問題。(例如:Y 市場準備好讓我們的客戶進入了嗎?)
  • 驗證一個假設。(例如:「法規是我們客戶推出 X 產品的主要障礙」)

人的做法

在顧問業,我們一般用這幾個步驟來解這兩類問題:

  1. 定義問題
  2. 拆解問題結構
  3. 排序議題優先級
  4. 設計分析
  5. 執行分析
  6. 綜整發現
  7. 提出建議

(Google 搜尋「McKinsey 7-step problem solving」可看到更多,網路上有不少不錯的說明,例如 slideworks.io/resources/mckinsey-problem-solving-process)

AI 的做法

一家公司會只因為 Opus 4.8 說要做,就把 $500M 砸進一個新產品上市嗎?我不認為。所以我覺得差距只歸結於一件事:信任。給客戶的建議是由 McKinsey、BCG 這類顧問公司提供的,他們有自己的聲譽與可信度。因此,AI 的產出需要由有本事的顧問來校對、詮釋、打磨。

所以人的判斷在這裡仍是 KSF(關鍵成功因素);我想設計一套遵循「人的做法」、在我工作中當助手的 workflow。

產品論點與範圍

痛點:我不信任 AI 的產出,因為它的推理過程是個黑箱。

解法:把那個黑箱拆成人看得到、能審核的步驟,然後讓 AI 去做研究。

範圍:我提過我的任務是「驗證假設」與「替模糊問題找答案」。但這個產品,我只專注在「驗證假設」,也就是輸入會是陳述(statement),不是問題(question)——因為這兩個任務的範圍差很多。回答問題需要澄清、需要跟真人來回很多次,所以我只專注在拆解一個陳述、找出它背後的真與假。

第二步:開煮

把第 1 步的所有脈絡貼進 Claude Code,叫它把這個產品做出來。一測,三個問題就冒出來:

問題

1. 要回答的問題,其分解/定義不夠具體,或我不同意它。

例如:

問題陳述:Y 市場準備好讓我們客戶進入了嗎?

AI 的問題分解:

  • 市場夠大嗎?
  • 市場可滲透嗎?
  • 滲透率預測

我的評論:

  • 乍看合理,但預測的範圍是模糊的。
  • 對我來說,預測滲透率就等於回答了那兩個問題(市場規模 & 滲透率)。
  • 除了規模和 % 預測,回答「Y 市場準備好讓我們客戶進入了嗎?」還有質性因素,例如:法規?機會成本?

2. 證據是假的。(幻覺 URL)

3. 證據是真的,但不夠全面。(例如,營收成長不等於獲利成長,但 AI 可能抓到營收成長的數據,就得出「獲利成長」的結論。)

解法

問題 1 的解法:

硬道理:推理上,我認為我還是比 AI 強。就算不是,AI 也還是得說服我;所以無論誰比較強,AI 跟我都需要對齊(alignment)。

解法:做一個高互動的介面;逐層分解,每一層都要我批准才能繼續。

  • 做一個 mindset.md,介紹顧問解問題的方法(即 issue tree、hypo tree 等等)。
  • 做一個 memory.md,把 casebook 倒進檔案裡,訓練 AI 像我一樣推理,並跟我試跑幾輪。
  • 做一個 lesson.md,一個學習迴圈,但不是 append-only 的檔案。每次人類修正後,記下心法與原則,讓它保持精簡,避免它變成一份無限增長的 log。

問題 2:

初步想法:加一步,逐一驗證每個 URL 的真實性,並逐字核對它的引文。

測試後:我拿一個真實論點(「遠距工作降低生產力」)來跑,結果一個 PubMed 來源回報「已失效」,不是因為它是假的,而是因為 PubMed 擋爬蟲。Naive 的驗證差點殺掉一個真實的 Tier-A 學術來源。

修正:用「加上類瀏覽器的 header、宣告失效前先重試鏡像來源」來區分「網站擋爬蟲」(PubMed、doi.org)與「真的不存在」。教訓:「我抓不到」≠「它不存在」,而這個錯,在最權威的來源上最危險。

問題 3:

解法很簡單:強制答案只能用來源的逐字原文,禁止任何改寫。

最終產品

那個我做了、卻又砍掉的模組

另外,我想分享一個我一路上學到的教訓。

我的思考停在「設計輸入」、然後讓 AI 去做研究。這讓我不小心設計了一個 auto-rollup:AI 讀完所有判定與研究結論,把它們捲成一個總體答案。這個摘要,在結尾又重建了另一個黑箱。

取而代之的:把它設成只允許有效的 URL 與真實引文;禁止改寫。改成我加了一套信心評分系統,讓 AI 替每個答案標上「它認為自己的答案有多相關、是否真能回答我的問題」的信心值。雖然這也是個黑箱過程,但這些我自己讀,而信心指數只是更幫我辨認哪裡可能出錯。

結論:Delete-first(先刪)。Elon Musk 在一次訪談裡分享過這個 sauce。把你的工作拆成 atomic module,然後試著把它們全部刪掉。只有當「沒有這個模組,產品會不會垮?」的答案是 YES 時,才把它加回來。這在我的 vibecoding 旅程裡極度有用,因為我覺得,隨著 /plan 和 /ultraplan 變主流、加上 vibecoding,AI 有時會在你的設計裡塞一些很酷的功能,但也塞一些很炫的 BS。

學到的一課

你必須知道你在做什麼。你是 AI 迴圈裡的把關者。否則,這個迴圈就只是 garbage in、更大的 garbage out。/plan 和 /ultraplan 是很強的指令,但如果你跟它沒有對齊,AI 會包一些 BS、然後把它叫做一個 feature。

Delete-first。把你的工作計畫拆成 atomic 的工作模組。試著刪掉每一個,只有在「沒它產品就動不了」時才加回來。讓流程盡可能精簡。別在 GitHub 上裝一堆花俏的 CLAUDE.md 或 skill。只有在你清楚知道自己要它幹嘛時才裝;到那時你會發現,你乾脆自己做一個還比較好。GitHub repo 就像 Pinterest——它們給你靈感,不是一個免費即插即用的工具。

能替客戶 ship、iterate、長出 AI 產品/workflow 的顧問,才是顧問業的未來。我們對組織有一個總覽視角,知道該做什麼、為何做、解決什麼問題。這些都遠比「做」本身重要。
