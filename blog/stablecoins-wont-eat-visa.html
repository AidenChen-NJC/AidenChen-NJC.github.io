Why Stablecoins Won't Eat Visa

Citrini Research said stablecoins would disintermediate the card networks. Visa and Mastercard sold off on the news. The thesis is intuitive — and wrong about how payments actually work.

Citrini Research published The 2028 Global Intelligence Crisis on Feb 22, 2026, arguing that stablecoins would disintermediate the card networks. Visa and Mastercard sold off on the news.

They're Wrong

The argument is intuitive: payment chains are crowded with intermediaries, blockchains can eliminate the middle layer, so crypto-native payments should — in theory — route around Visa, Mastercard, and the rest.

What That Argument Ignores

  • Consumers don't feel the cost. Fees sit on the merchant side and get baked into prices. No one switches payment tools to chase a cost they can't see.
  • Visa and Mastercard can just adopt it. Their bottleneck is regulatory frameworks, not technology. They already have users, UX, and trust. Crypto-native rails start from zero.
  • Crypto UX still isn't ready. Wallet setup, merchants needing wallets, manual transfers, no chargebacks, no card perks. The average consumer has no reason to learn any of it.

And the Networks Have Already Moved

  • Visa: crypto card transaction volume grew 525% in 2025, now supports stablecoin settlement across four chains, and stood up a Stablecoins Advisory Practice in December 2025.
  • Mastercard: MTN has signed partnerships with Standard Chartered and JPMorgan Kinexys, and joined Paxos's Global Dollar Network.
  • Stripe: acquired Bridge for $1.1B, launched its own payments chain Tempo, and now lets enterprises issue their own stablecoins in days.

This isn't disruption. It's integration. Blockchain will only kill the players that refuse to adopt it.

Payments will end as a UX game.
